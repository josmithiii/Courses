The music signal processing book series now has a self-paced course for each of its four books. An AI tutor takes you through each book one idea at a time, checks every step with a small exercise, and remembers where you left off.

By Julius O. Smith III. The books grew out of courses taught at CCRMA since 1984, written to start from a good high-school math background.

Spectral track
Book I · mdft

Mathematics of the DFT

Starts from high-school algebra and trigonometry. Complex numbers, Euler's identity, sinusoids, signals as vectors, the DFT, Fourier theorems, practical spectrum analysis.

The DFT is a change of coordinates: it projects a signal onto sampled sinusoids that are all perpendicular to each other.

Why does fft([1, 0, −1, 0]) give two peaks for one cosine, and why are they 2 tall? You work it out by hand at N = 4, then at N = 64 in Python.

Book IV · sasp

Spectral Audio Signal Processing

Builds on the DFT course. Windows, spectrum analysis of sinusoids and noise, the STFT, overlap-add and filter banks, sinusoidal modeling, the phase vocoder, multirate filter banks, MP3's filter bank.

Every sinusoid shows up in a spectrum as the shape of the window, and the STFT can be read two ways: as overlapping frames or as a bank of filters.

To tell A4 from A♯4 apart, a Hann window must be at least 153 ms long. You find out why, then hear it.

Physical track
Book II · filters

Introduction to Digital Filters

Starts from high-school algebra and trigonometry. Difference equations, the z transform, frequency and phase response, poles and zeros, audio EQ filters, minimum phase, IIR design, state space, Faust.

Where a filter's poles and zeros sit relative to the unit circle decides everything it does to a sound.

y(n) = x(n) + x(n−1) is a lowpass filter. By the end you can sketch any filter's response from its pole-zero plot, and hear that you were right.

Book III · pasp

Physical Audio Signal Processing

Builds on the filters course and draws on the whole series: the DFT throughout, and the spectral course's perceptual analysis for calibrating models against real instruments. Delay effects, artificial reverberation, digital waveguides, scattering, wave digital filters, finite differences, virtual guitars, pianos, reeds, bowed strings and brass.

A traveling wave is a delay line, and waves partly reflect wherever the impedance changes.

A plucked A440 string is 99.727 samples of delay and one tiny filter. You build it, predict how long each harmonic rings, and listen.

A session

About an hour, one idea at a time

Each session recaps the last idea and asks one recall question. Then it teaches one new idea, picture first, and doesn't move on until a small exercise shows it has landed. Exercises are done by hand, in a few lines of Python, or picked from the book's own exercise bank.

Every topic links to its page in the free online book. Your progress and a log of each session stay in a folder on your own computer.

Tutor

Last time you projected [3, 1] onto [1, 1] and [1, −1]. What were the two coefficients?

You

2 and 1. So [3, 1] = 2·[1, 1] + 1·[1, −1].

Tutor

Exactly. Here's the surprise: you just computed a length-2 DFT. Today we do the same thing with four arrows instead of two. Try ⟨x, s₁⟩ for x = [1, 0, −1, 0] and s₁ = [1, j, −1, −j]. Remember to conjugate s₁.

Get started

Three steps

1

Get Claude

Install Claude Code. It runs in a terminal and in the Claude desktop app's Code tab, and needs a Claude account.

2

Add the courses

Paste the line below into a terminal, once. It installs all the courses for both the terminal and the desktop app.

3

Say what you want

Start a session in any folder and ask for a lesson by name. On the first lesson the tutor asks where to keep your progress.

claude plugin marketplace add https://github.com/josmithiii/Courses && claude plugin install courses@josmithiii-courses

Then say let's do today's mdft lesson, or filters, sasp, pasp.

The books and the courses are free. The tutor runs on your own Claude account. Prefer a clone? The course repository has instructions for running a course with ./take mdft.

About the books

The four books began as readers for CCRMA's music signal processing courses, first written for entering Music Ph.D. students. The only prerequisite was a good high-school math background, and the books build everything else along the way. All four are free online and in print.

About the courses

Each course follows its book chapter by chapter, pins every new idea to one worked example you come back to again and again, and ends with a frontier section that is updated as research moves on. No course is a hard prerequisite for another: if you hit a gap, the tutor points you to the exact topic to review.
