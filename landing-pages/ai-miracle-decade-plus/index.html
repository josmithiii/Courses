AI track · paper reading · free

Forty papers that made modern AI.

Read the landmark AI papers from 2006 to 2023, DBN to GPT-4, in roughly the order the field lived them. A tutor frames each paper before you read it, asks what surprised you afterward, and places it in the story.

For curious technical readers who want the actual papers, not summaries. Math is explained where it carries the argument. No PyTorch needed; runnable demos are optional.

The route

Twelve phases, forty papers, no time limit

Most phases end at a concept stop: an essay that ties their papers together along one of seven threads, such as the pretrain-and-fine-tune lineage and scaling and emergence. Stops are stops, not days. Spend an hour on one or three weeks.

  1. 0Orientation. The whole arc in one sitting, and how to read an AI paper without drowning.
  2. 1The restart and the cash-out (2006-2012). DBN, AlexNet.
  3. 2The machinery years (2013-2015). word2vec, VAE, GANs, Seq2Seq, Adam, batch norm, dropout, U-Net, ResNet.
  4. 3Deep RL comes of age (2013-2017). DQN, AlphaGo, AlphaZero.
  5. 4Sequences, audio, and the attention turn (2016-2017). WaveNet, the Transformer, VQ-VAE.
  6. 5Pretrain and fine-tune (2018-2019). ELMo, GPT-1, BERT, GPT-2.
  7. 6Scaling and emergence (2020). Scaling laws, GPT-3.
  8. 7Diffusion, ViT, text-to-image (2020-2022). DDPM, ViT, DALL-E, CLIP, latent diffusion, Imagen.
  9. 8The post-Chinchilla LLM era (2022). Chinchilla, PaLM, chain-of-thought, retrieval.
  10. 9Alignment (2022). InstructGPT, Constitutional AI.
  11. 10Capabilities and the frontier (2022-2023). Whisper, AlphaFold 2, Toolformer, LLaMA, GPT-4.
  12. 11Synthesis. Write your own "what's dead, what's alive" note, then pick a post-2023 paper.

One stop

How a paper is read

  1. 1Frame.Why this paper, what came before it, and what problem it tried to solve.
  2. 2Read.You read the paper or the key sections, on arXiv or at the publisher.
  3. 3Discuss.One or two questions: what surprised you, and what is the claim everything rests on?
  4. 4Place.The tutor puts the paper on its thread, connecting it to the papers before and after.
  5. 5Optionally, do.A small exercise, a runnable demo, or a "what's dead, what's alive" verdict.
  6. 6Log.Notes and open questions go to your progress file, kept on your own computer.

Side quests are encouraged. When a paper hooks you, follow it: read the citing work, watch a lecture, run a model. The tutor logs the detour and brings you back to the route afterward, so no landmark gets skipped.

Get started

Three steps

1

Get Claude

Install Claude Code. It runs in a terminal and in the Claude desktop app's Code tab, and needs a Claude account.

2

Add the courses

Paste the line below into a terminal, once. It installs all the courses for both the terminal and the desktop app.

3

Ask for the next stop

Start a session in any folder and ask for the next session. On the first one the tutor asks where to keep your progress.

claude plugin marketplace add https://github.com/josmithiii/Courses && claude plugin install courses@josmithiii-courses

Then say let's do today's ai-miracle-decade-plus lesson.
